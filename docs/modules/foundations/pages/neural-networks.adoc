= 1.3 Neural networks
:description: A neuron is a weighted sum followed by a squash. A network is many neurons in layers.

.What you will learn
* What one artificial neuron computes.
* How neurons stack into layers and networks.
* Why depth lets a network learn complex things.

== One neuron

An artificial neuron does three things:

. Multiplies each input by a *weight*.
. Adds the results, plus one extra number called the *bias*.
. Passes the sum through an *activation function*.

Should you take an umbrella? Two inputs: is it cloudy (1 or 0), and the
forecast chance of rain (0 to 1).

[source,text]
----
sum = cloudy × 0.4 + rain_chance × 2.0 + bias (−1.0)

cloudy = 1, rain_chance = 0.7  →  0.4 + 1.4 − 1.0 = 0.8
----

The weights say how much each input matters: rain chance matters five times
more than clouds. The bias sets how easily the neuron fires.

The activation function then turns the sum into an output. A common one,
the *sigmoid*, squashes any number into the range 0 to 1:

[source,text]
----
sigmoid(0.8) ≈ 0.69   → 69% "take the umbrella"
----

The weights and the bias are the neuron's *parameters*. Learning means finding
good values for them.

== Layers

One neuron can only draw a straight line through the data. Real problems need
more, so neurons are stacked:

[source,text]
----
inputs        hidden layer       hidden layer       output
  x1 ──┐      ┌─ n ─┐            ┌─ n ─┐
  x2 ──┼──▶   ├─ n ─┼──▶         ├─ n ─┼──▶          y
  x3 ──┘      └─ n ─┘            └─ n ─┘
----

* The *input layer* receives the features.
* Each *hidden layer* takes the previous layer's outputs as its inputs.
* The *output layer* gives the prediction.

Every connection has its own weight. A network with 1,000 inputs and a hidden
layer of 1,000 neurons already has a million weights in that one layer.

== Why depth works

Each layer builds on the one before. In an image network, early layers learn to
detect edges, middle layers combine edges into shapes such as eyes or wheels,
and late layers combine shapes into objects such as faces or cars. Nobody
programs those detectors. They emerge from training.

The activation function is what makes this possible. Without it, stacking
layers would collapse into one big weighted sum, still a straight line. The
squash adds the bends that let a deep network fit curved, complicated patterns.

"Deep learning" just means networks with many layers.

.Key ideas
****
* Neuron: weighted sum of inputs, plus bias, through an activation function.
* Weights and biases are the parameters.
* Layers feed into layers; depth builds complex features from simple ones.
* Activation functions are what make depth useful.
****

== Check yourself

. A neuron has weights 0.5 and −1.0, bias 0.2, and inputs 2 and 1. What is
  the sum before activation?
. What would happen if a network had no activation functions?
. Where do the "edge detectors" in an image network come from?

[%collapsible]
.Answers
====
. 2 × 0.5 + 1 × (−1.0) + 0.2 = 0.2.
. All the layers would collapse into one linear function: no more powerful
  than a single layer.
. Training. They emerge because they help reduce the error; nobody writes them.
====
