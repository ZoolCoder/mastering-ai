= 1.5 The road to LLMs
:description: From hand-written rules to ChatGPT and coding agents: the six steps that got us here.

.What you will learn
* The main milestones of modern AI, and why each one mattered.
* Why "scale" became the central idea.

== Six steps

[cols="1,2,3"]
|===
| When | Milestone | Why it mattered

| 1950s–1980s
| Rule-based AI and expert systems
| Hand-written rules. Impressive in narrow areas, brittle everywhere else.

| 1986
| Backpropagation popularised
| Made it practical to train networks with hidden layers (lesson 1.4).

| 2012
| AlexNet wins ImageNet
| A deep network trained on GPUs crushed the image-recognition benchmark.
Deep learning took over.

| 2017
| The Transformer ("Attention Is All You Need")
| A new architecture that reads a whole sequence at once and trains well in
parallel. Every modern LLM is built on it. Part 2 explains it.

| 2018–2022
| GPT-1, 2, 3 and scaling laws
| Bigger models, trained on more data with more compute, got predictably
better. New abilities appeared at scale.

| 2022–today
| ChatGPT, then tools, reasoning and agents
| Assistant training made LLMs useful to everyone. Then models learned to call
tools, think before answering, and act in loops: agents.
|===

== Scale

The surprise of the last decade: the same simple recipe, a Transformer trained
to predict the next word, keeps getting better when you make three things
bigger together:

* *Parameters*: the size of the model.
* *Data*: how much text it trains on.
* *Compute*: how many chip-hours go into training.

These *scaling laws* turned AI from a research problem into an engineering and
investment race.

== Where this course goes next

Part 2 opens the box: how an LLM turns text into numbers, predicts the next
word, and becomes an assistant. Parts 3 to 6 show how to use and build on it.
Parts 7 and 8 cover the newest layer: agents that act, and the harnesses that
make them reliable.

.Key ideas
****
* Rules gave way to learning; learning gave way to deep learning on GPUs.
* The Transformer (2017) is the architecture behind every modern LLM.
* Scale of parameters, data and compute drove most of the progress.
* The newest step is agents: models that use tools in a loop.
****
