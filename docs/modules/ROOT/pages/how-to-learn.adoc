= How to use this course
:description: How lessons, checks and labs fit together, and what you need before you start.

== How a lesson works

Every lesson has the same shape:

. *What you will learn*: two to four goals, so you know what to look for.
. *The lesson*: short sections, each with one idea.
. *Key ideas*: the lesson in five lines. Reread these before the next lesson.
. *Check yourself*: a few questions. Answer them in your head or on paper
  before you open the answer.

Each part ends with a *lab*: a small program you build and run yourself. The
labs live in the {labs-url}[`labs/` folder] of the repository.

== What you need

|===

| 4–6

| 7–8

to practise on.

| 9–11
| What you used above. Nothing new to buy.
|===

== Five habits that make it stick

. *Type the code, do not paste it.* Your fingers notice details your eyes skip.
. *Predict before you run.* Write down what you expect. Being wrong is where
  the learning is.
. *Ask a model, then check.* Use an AI assistant to explain what you do not
  understand, then verify the answer against the lesson. That is part 9's
  "discernment" in practice.
. *Explain it to someone.* If you cannot explain a lesson in two minutes, read
  its key ideas again.
. *Build something of your own* after each part, however small.

== Pace

The full course is about 60 to 80 hours. At five hours a week that is three to
four months. You do not need all of it: xref:paths.adoc[] lists shorter
routes for different goals.

NOTE: The field moves fast. Concepts in parts 1 to 3 change slowly. Product
names and features in parts 7 and 10 change every few months. Each page shows
when it was last updated.
